How a drive happens

From idea to score

Someone proposes it

Any member can propose a drive on our Volunteer Hub. Someone from the core team reviews it and approves it.

The plan is locked

Before the day, the organiser writes down the target, the budget and how many volunteers they need. After that, it can't be changed.

Members sign up

Everyone gets a notification, and volunteering takes one tap.

The day itself

The organiser takes attendance, keeps every receipt and counts the people helped.

Hours are verified

Volunteers log their hours. They only count once the organiser checks them against attendance.

The drive is scored

We compare what happened with the plan. That comparison is the drive's EDPI score.

How we measure a drive

Every drive gets a score

It's called EDPI, the ElevateX Drive Performance Index. It compares what a drive actually did with the plan that was locked before it started. A drive that does exactly what it planned scores 100. More volunteers than expected, or working with an outside partner, can take it up to 115.

A score under 100 isn't a fail. It shows the team which part of the drive to talk about in the debrief, and over time it tells us whether our planning is getting better.

Impact50%

Did we hit the target we set: meals served, kilos collected, saplings planted?

Money25%

How much did we spend per person helped, and did we stay inside the budget?

Volunteers25%

Did enough people turn up, and was the work shared fairly?
